IVACK University Learning management system(Portal)

 1. Who might be the main target audience for this project, and what are their needs?  
Target Audience:  
- Students: Need a platform to share academic materials, collaborate on projects, and receive updates from lecturers.  
- Lecturers/Professors: Need a way to distribute course materials, announcements, and resources to specific classes or departments efficiently.  
- Visitors (Prospective Students, Researchers, etc.): Need access to general information about the institution, admission opportunities, and public academic content.  

Their Needs:  
- Students want an easy way to upload and access study materials, engage with peers, and stay updated on academic notices.  
- Lecturers need a structured way to share files, videos, and announcements with the right audience (e.g., a single class or the whole department).  
- Visitors require a user-friendly way to explore the institution’s offerings and apply for programs.  

 2. What are the obstacles stopping them from reaching their goals right now?  
- Fragmented Communication: Many institutions rely on email, physical notices, or multiple platforms (e.g., LMS, social media), making it hard to centralize information.  
- Limited Access Control: Lecturers may struggle to share materials with specific groups (e.g., only a particular class) without spamming everyone.  
- Poor File Management: Students and lecturers often deal with disorganized file-sharing systems (e.g., WhatsApp, Google Drive links) that lack proper categorization.  
- Visitor Accessibility: Prospective students may find it difficult to navigate institutional websites to find relevant opportunities.  

 3. How would you go about getting this information?  
To validate these insights, I could:  
- Conduct User Interviews: Talk to students, lecturers, and admin staff to understand their pain points with current systems.  
- Surveys & Feedback Forms: Distribute questionnaires to gather quantitative data on common frustrations.  
- Competitor Analysis: Study existing platforms (e.g., Moodle, Blackboard) to see where they fall short.  
- Usability Testing: Let real users interact with your prototype (like your live demo) and observe where they struggle.  
- Analytics Review: If your site is already live, track user behavior (e.g., which features are used most, where users drop off).
